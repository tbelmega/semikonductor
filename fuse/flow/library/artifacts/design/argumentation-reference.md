# Argumentation reference

A primer on argumentation for checking decision justifications in a design document.
`adversarial-review.md` next to this file uses it for its fallacy checking. It covers the Toulmin
model, the common fallacies, a scoring rubric for a justification, and what to ask the author for
at each score.

## Toulmin model

Every argument has six components:

- **Claim**: the conclusion being argued.
- **Data or grounds**: the evidence supporting the claim.
- **Warrant**: the logical bridge from the data to the claim.
- **Backing**: support for the warrant itself.
- **Qualifier**: the degree of certainty, such as always, usually or presumably.
- **Rebuttal**: the conditions under which the claim does not hold.

A strong argument makes all six components explicit. A weak argument omits the warrant or the
backing.

## Fallacy taxonomy

### Relevance fallacies

- **Ad hominem**: attacking the person, not the argument.
- **Appeal to authority**: citing an authority without evidence.
- **Appeal to popularity**: "everyone does it".
- **Red herring**: distracting with irrelevant information.
- **Straw man**: misrepresenting the opposing argument.

### Presumption fallacies

- **Begging the question**: the conclusion is assumed in a premise.
- **False dichotomy**: presenting only two options when more exist.
- **Slippery slope**: assuming one event inevitably leads to another.
- **Hasty generalization**: drawing broad conclusions from few examples.

### Ambiguity fallacies

- **Equivocation**: using a word with two meanings interchangeably.
- **Amphiboly**: an ambiguous grammatical structure.

### Formal fallacies

- **Affirming the consequent**: if P then Q; Q; therefore P.
- **Denying the antecedent**: if P then Q; not P; therefore not Q.

## Scoring rubric

Score each criterion from 0 to 3, for a maximum of 15.

| Criterion               | 0              | 1         | 2                 | 3                |
| ----------------------- | -------------- | --------- | ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Claim clarity           | Absent         | Vague     | Clear             | Precise, scoped  |
| Evidence quality        | None           | Anecdotal | Documented        | Primary source   |
| Warrant validity        | Missing        | Implied   | Stated            | Logically sound  |
| Rebuttal acknowledgment | None           | Dismissed | Acknowledged      | Addressed        |
| Internal consistency    | Contradictions | Gaps      | Mostly consistent | Fully consistent |

Tiers: 0 to 5 beginner, 6 to 9 intermediate, 10 to 12 advanced, 13 to 15 competitive.

## What to ask for at each score

- Below 6: rebuild from the claim. "What exactly are you claiming, and what evidence supports it?"
- 6 to 9: strengthen the warrant. "Why does this evidence support this conclusion?"
- 10 to 12: address rebuttals. "Under what conditions would this argument fail?"
- 13 to 15: challenge the backing. "What supports your warrant itself?"
